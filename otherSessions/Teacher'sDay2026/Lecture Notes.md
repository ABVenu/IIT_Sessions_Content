# AI in Daily Life

## What You Will Learn in This Session

This masterclass is an introduction to **Artificial Intelligence**. You do not need a laptop, and you do not need any coding.

You already use AI on a phone. The work today is to **see** it, **name** it, and **use** it as a study helper.

In this session, you will:

- Spot AI already on a phone: **finger unlock**, **face unlock**, and **word prediction**
- Tell the difference between **this is AI** and **this is not AI**
- Watch the **elephant video** and see how a camera can recognise an animal — not just “something moved”
- Watch live demonstrations of **Google Gemini**, **Google Lens**, **NotebookLM**, **Google Stitch**, and a small **school to-do app** built on Lovable or Bolt

By the end, you will look at a phone feature and say “this guesses from examples” or “this only follows a fixed rule.” You will also know how to ask AI for help with school work without copying.

## What Is Artificial Intelligence

People hear the word **AI** in news, YouTube, and WhatsApp forwards. The word sounds large. The idea is smaller than it looks.

- **Official Definition:** **Artificial Intelligence (AI)** is the ability of a computer or a phone to do tasks that usually need human thinking — such as recognising a face, guessing the next word, or answering a question in ordinary language.
- **In Simple Words:** AI is a helper inside an app. It looks at many examples, notices a **pattern**, and then makes a useful **guess**.
- **Real-Life Example:** After you type `I am going to`, the keyboard may suggest `school`. Nobody is sitting inside the phone. The keyboard has seen that sentence ending many times.

**Need:** If you think AI is only a robot in a film, you will miss the AI that already unlocks your phone.

**Logic:** A **pattern** is “what usually comes next.” AI is strong at that kind of guess. It is not a person, and it does not care about you the way a teacher does.

**Common doubt:** *“Is every smart machine AI?”* No. A machine can be useful and still follow a fixed rule — a calculator is fast at arithmetic, and that still is not AI.

## AI Already on Your Phone

You do not have to download a new app to meet AI. Three features on almost every smartphone are enough to start.

![A smartphone held over a school desk, with three panels labelled Finger Unlock, Face Unlock, and Word Prediction showing next-word chips going, to, school](https://s13n-curr-images-bucket.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/othersessions/teachers-day-2026/teachersday2026-01-ai-on-your-phone.png?v=20260903)

### Finger Unlock

- **Official Definition:** **Finger unlock** (fingerprint unlock) is a phone check that matches the pattern of your fingertip with the pattern saved on that phone.
- **In Simple Words:** Your finger is the key. The phone has learned the lines on *your* finger, not on your friend’s finger.
- **Real-Life Example:** You press the sensor. The phone opens. Your classmate’s finger on the same phone does not open it.

**Need:** A PIN can be seen over your shoulder. A fingerprint is harder to copy in daily use.

**Common doubt:** *“Does the phone send my fingerprint to the internet?”* On most phones, the match happens **on the device**. Still, do not unlock a stranger’s phone with your finger, and do not share lock-screen tricks as a joke.

### Face Unlock

- **Official Definition:** **Face unlock** is a phone check that matches your face with the face stored when you first set up the phone.
- **In Simple Words:** The front camera looks at you and asks, “Is this the same person who owns this phone?”
- **Real-Life Example:** You pick up the phone in a bright classroom. It unlocks when it sees your face. It should not unlock for a printed photo of you — good phones are trained to notice a real face.

**Logic:** At setup, the phone stores many views of your face. Later it **compares**, it does not “know” you like a friend knows you.

**Common doubt:** *“If I wear a mask, why does it fail?”* The stored pattern includes your mouth and nose, so covering them makes the guess weak. That failure is useful: the phone is matching a pattern, not reading your mind.

### Word Prediction

- **Official Definition:** **Word prediction** is a keyboard feature that suggests the next word (or a spelling) from patterns in language.
- **In Simple Words:** The keyboard tries to finish your sentence.
- **Real-Life Example:** You type `I am` and see chips such as `going`, `to`, `school`. You tap instead of typing every letter.

**Need:** Many students think faster than they type. Word prediction and **voice typing** save time in WhatsApp and in search.

**Common doubt:** *“Why does it suggest a word I never wanted?”* Because it guesses what is **common**, not what is **true for you today**. If you were writing about a hospital visit, it might still suggest `school`. That is a good reminder: AI can be confident and still be wrong.

2. The phone is locked, then it unlocks with **face unlock**.
3. Messages or WhatsApp opens, someone types `I am`, and the keyboard waits.

Write in your notebook the **three suggested words** you see. Those three words are AI making a guess in front of you.

## This Is AI and This Is Not AI

The next skill is a filter. Not every app on a phone is AI. If you cannot tell the difference, every tool will feel like magic, and magic is hard to use well.

**Rule in one line:** If the tool **learns from examples** and then **guesses**, it is behaving like AI. If it only follows a **fixed rule** that never changes, it is not AI.

![Two-column classroom poster: This is AI lists finger unlock, face unlock, keyboard suggestions, next-video list, and map traffic; This is not AI lists a calculator, a simple paint app, an alarm clock, and a torch](https://s13n-curr-images-bucket.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/othersessions/teachers-day-2026/teachersday2026-02-ai-vs-not-ai.png?v=20260903)

### This is AI

These tools change their guess when the examples change.

| What you see | What the phone is guessing |
|---|---|
| **Finger unlock** | “Is this the same fingertip I stored?” |
| **Face unlock** | “Is this the same face I stored?” |
| **Word prediction** | “Which word usually comes next?” |
| **YouTube “Up next”** | “Which video do people like you watch after this one?” |
| **Google Maps traffic colours** | “Which road is slow *right now*, from many phones on that road?” |
| **Spam SMS / spam call flags** | “Does this message look like the junk we have seen before?” |
| **Google Photos grouping faces** | “Which photos look like the same person?” |
| **Google Lens** | “What does this picture match — text, a plant, a place?” |

Maps is a clear city example. The red and orange lines are not painted by a person sitting in Bengaluru traffic. Many phones on the road send a speed signal, and the app **guesses** which route is faster.

### This is not AI

These tools are useful. They still are not AI.

| What you see | Why it is not AI |
|---|---|
| **Calculator** | `2 + 2` is always `4`. It does not learn your style of maths. |
| **Simple Paint / drawing app** | You draw a circle. The app only stores the colour you put on the screen. |
| **Alarm clock** | You set 5:30. It rings at 5:30. It does not guess a new time. |
| **Torch / flashlight** | On or off. No pattern. No guess. |
| **Basic notepad** | It stores the letters you type. It does not suggest the next sentence unless a separate AI keyboard is on. |
| **Ceiling fan remote** | Speed 1, 2, 3. Fixed buttons. |

**Common doubt:** *“The calculator is faster than me. Is that not intelligence?”* Speed is not AI — a fan is fast at moving air, and it still does not learn.

**Common doubt:** *“Some paint apps now make pictures from a sentence.”* That extra feature is AI. The **simple** paint app that only has brush and colour is not.

### What the video shows

- **Official Definition:** **E-Eye** is an AI system that watches railway cameras, recognises an elephant from learned pictures, and can send a warning without a person staring at every screen.
- **In Simple Words:** The camera does not only say “something moved.” It tries to say **what** moved.
- **Real-Life Example:** Face unlock asks “is this *my* face?” E-Eye asks “is this an *elephant*?” Both match a new picture to a pattern they already learned.

The system has seen many pictures of elephants — size, shape, and walking style. When a new shape appears, it **guesses** “this looks like an elephant” and can warn the driver.

### What a simple rule would have done

A simple sensor can only follow a fixed rule: “something large moved — switch on a light.” That rule cannot tell an **elephant** from a truck or a bag of debris.

**Need:** On a railway, a wrong guess can cost lives — of people and of animals. A calculator being wrong is only a wrong sum.

**Logic:** A traditional light can fire for any large object. AI adds the extra step: **name the object**, then alert a human.

**Common doubt:** *“Does a person sit and watch every camera?”* No. The system watches first. People still decide what to do with the warning.

Keep this video in mind. Breast-scan helpers, car cameras that notice a cow, and buses that slow down use the **same idea**: look at many pictures, then warn a person.

## How AI Makes a Guess

The elephant video is the method. The phone examples were the same method in your pocket.

- **Official Definition:** AI **learns from examples**: it is shown many cases (faces, elephants, sentences, roads), stores the pattern, and later guesses a new case.
- **In Simple Words:** Show many. Notice the pattern. Guess the next one.
- **Real-Life Example:** A kirana shopkeeper who has seen you buy milk every evening may keep a packet ready. That is a human guess from examples. Word prediction and E-Eye are the same idea inside a machine.

AI does not “understand” photosynthesis the way your science teacher does. It has read many explanations, so it can **write something that looks like** an explanation.

**Need:** This is why AI can sound sure and still be wrong. A confident sentence is not the same as a checked fact. An animal box on a camera can also be wrong — the driver still looks at the road.

**Logic:** If the examples were messy, the guess can be messy. If your **question** is messy, the answer will be messy too — that is the bridge to the next topic.

**Common doubt:** *“So is AI just copy-paste?”* Sometimes it repeats common lines. Sometimes it mixes patterns in a new order. Either way, **you** must check it against the textbook and the teacher.

### Google Gemini (or Google search with an AI answer)

- **Official Definition:** **Google Gemini** is Google’s AI chat. You type or speak a question. It writes a reply.
- **In Simple Words:** It is a study partner you can ask in **English or Kannada**.
- **Real-Life Example:** “I am a Class 10 student. Explain photosynthesis in 6 short points. Then give 3 exam-style questions.”

Open it from the **Google app** or **gemini.google.com** on the phone browser. A school Google account is enough for the basic use shown today.

### Google Lens

- **Official Definition:** **Google Lens** is a camera tool that reads what it sees — printed text, a plant, a board, a product.
- **In Simple Words:** Point the camera. Ask “what is this?”
- **Real-Life Example:** Point at a Kannada paragraph in the textbook and ask for English. Point at a leaf in the school garden and read the name.

On many Android phones, Lens is already inside **Google** or the **camera**. You can try this today on a parent’s phone without buying anything.

### Voice typing

- **Official Definition:** **Voice typing** (on Gboard and similar keyboards) turns your speech into text.
- **In Simple Words:** You speak. The phone types.
- **Real-Life Example:** Speak a long WhatsApp message in Kannada instead of hunting for letters.

**Need:** If typing in English feels slow, speaking is a fair way in. The AI still has to hear you clearly. A quiet corner helps.

### Google Translate

- **Official Definition:** **Google Translate** converts text or speech from one language to another.
- **In Simple Words:** Kannada to English, English to Kannada, for a notice, a question, or a YouTube title.
- **Real-Life Example:** A school circular in English. You photograph it or type a line, and read it in Kannada first.

### YouTube and Google Maps — use them with open eyes

You already open these apps. The new habit is to notice the **guess**.

- YouTube is not only “the video I chose.” The **next** video is a suggestion. Search with a **chapter name**, not only with whatever appears first.
- Maps is not only “the blue line.” The line changes because traffic data changes. That is AI helping you reach school or a bus stop.

**Common doubt:** *“Do I need ChatGPT?”* No. For daily doubts on an Indian phone, **Gemini + Lens + Translate + voice typing** is enough to start. The next three tools are extra helpers for **slides, posters, and a small school to-do app**.

### NotebookLM

- **Official Definition:** **NotebookLM** is a Google study tool. You put in your notes or a chapter. It can turn that material into a **study guide**, **slides**, or a short **audio** recap.
- **In Simple Words:** You give it *your* chapter. It helps you revise *that* chapter — not a random Chapter 12 it invented.
- **Real-Life Example:** Paste or upload Class 10 notes on photosynthesis. Ask for a 6-slide revision deck. Open [notebooklm.google.com](https://notebooklm.google.com) later on a family phone or computer.

**Need:** Gemini can talk about a topic from memory. NotebookLM is stronger when the **real notes** are in front of it. That is why the vague “summarise Chapter 12 of my book” problem is smaller here.

**Common doubt:** *“Can I upload the whole textbook?”* Start with **one chapter** or your own notes. Check every slide against the book before you present.

### Google Stitch

- **Official Definition:** **Google Stitch** is a Google tool that draws a **first layout** from a short description — a poster, a pamphlet, or a simple app screen.
- **In Simple Words:** You say what the poster should say. It draws a first design. You still choose the words and the facts.
- **Real-Life Example:** “Science Day poster for Class 9, title Water Cycle, four labelled steps, school-notice style, English, no fake statistics.” Try it later at [stitch.withgoogle.com](https://stitch.withgoogle.com).

**Need:** A project poster is not “pick a nice colour.” The hard part is **clear title, correct steps, and readable text**. Stitch helps with the first layout. You still own the science.

**Common doubt:** *“Is the poster ready to pin on the board?”* No. Treat it as a **draft**. Fix spellings, add your name and class, and ask the teacher before you print.

## How to Ask a Clear Question

AI is not a mind-reader. A short, vague line gets a long, vague reply. A **clear question** gets something you can actually study from.

- **Official Definition:** A **prompt** is the question or instruction you type (or speak) to an AI chat.
- **In Simple Words:** A prompt is just **your ask**. Better ask, better help.
- **Real-Life Example:** “Explain photosynthesis” is a weak ask. “I am a Class 10 student. Explain photosynthesis in 6 short points. Then give 3 exam questions. Do not give the answers yet.” is a clear ask.

![A study tablet showing a weak question that gets a long unhelpful paragraph, a clear Class 10 question that gets six short points and exam questions, and a five-step strip labelled WHO, NEED, CLASS, LANGUAGE, FORMAT](https://s13n-curr-images-bucket.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/othersessions/teachers-day-2026/teachersday2026-03-clear-question.png?v=20260903)

Use this five-part strip every time. Write it on the inside cover of your notebook.

| Part | What you tell the AI | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Who** | Who you are | `I am a Class 10 student` |
| **Need** | What you want | `Explain photosynthesis` |
| **Class** | The level | `Class 10` / `Class 12` |
| **Language** | Kannada, English, or both | `Simple English, then Kannada words for new terms` |
| **Format** | How the answer should look | `6 short points, then 3 exam questions` |

**Need:** The same topic can be taught to Class 6 or Class 12. If you hide the class, AI may write like a college textbook.

**Logic:** **Format** is the part students skip. If you do not ask for short points, you may get a wall of text you will not reread.

**Common doubt:** *“Can I ask in Kannada?”* Yes. Try Kannada if English feels heavy. You can also ask: “Reply in simple English.”

### Demonstration: A Weak Question and a Clear Question

Watch both questions run on the screen, on the **same topic**.

**Question A (weak):**

```text
explain photosynthesis
```

**Question B (clear):**

```text
I am a Class 10 student in Bengaluru.
Explain photosynthesis in simple English in 6 short points.
Then give 3 exam-style questions.
Do not give the answers yet.
```

Notice three things while you watch:

- Which reply is **shorter to read**
- Which reply matches **Class 10**, not college
- Which reply you would actually **revise from** the night before a test

The tool did not become “smarter” between A and B. **Your question** did the work.

A second live pair you will see:

**Question C (weak):**

```text
help me with maths
```

**Question D (clear):**

```text
I am a Class 9 student.
Explain what a linear equation in one variable is, in simple English.
Give 4 practice questions.
Do not solve them.
```

“Help me with maths” has no chapter, no class, and no format. AI then guesses, and the guess may be the wrong chapter.

### Activity: Rewrite One Weak Question

Pick **one** topic from your current syllabus. In your notebook, write:

1. A **weak** one-line ask (like Question A)
2. A **clear** ask using Who, Need, Class, Language, Format

Keep it to the topic you are studying this week — Science, Maths, Social Science, or English. Do not write the AI’s answer. The skill today is the **question**.

**Suggested shape for Science:**

```text

Explain [topic] in 6 short points in simple English.
Give 3 exam-style questions.
Do not give the answers.
```

**Suggested shape for English:**

```text
I am a Class 11 student.
Here is my paragraph:
[paste 3 to 5 lines]
Correct grammar. List 5 changes in a table: wrong / right / why.
```

If you finish early, write a third line: **how you will check** the answer (textbook page, teacher, or a classmate).

## AI as a Study Helper, Not a Copy Machine

Teachers explain, check, and notice when you are stuck. AI can **practise with you**. It cannot replace a teacher.

That is the right picture on Teacher’s Day and on every other day.

**Good uses for school**

- “Explain this topic as if I missed class.”
- “Give me 5 extra questions. Do not give answers yet.”
- “Make a 10-line summary I can revise.”
- “Correct this English paragraph and tell me why.”
- “Give Kannada meanings for these 8 science words.”
- “Quiz me: ask one question, wait, then tell me if I am right.”

**Poor uses**

- “Write the full answers so I can copy them into my book.”
- “Finish this exam paper.”
- Pasting a personal story, your address, or a photo of an ID card

### Demonstration: An Answer You Must Check

Watch a question that is **too thin**. AI will still try to answer.

**Question E (dangerous because it is vague):**

```text
Give me the summary of Chapter 12 of my Science book.
```

The chat does not have **your** book on the table, so it may invent a Chapter 12. The sentences can look neat and still be the **wrong chapter**.

Then watch a repaired version:

**Question F:**

```text
I am a Class 10 student.
The topic is [exact topic name from the board, for example: Carbon and its Compounds].
Give a 10-line revision summary in simple English.
List 5 words I must know.
```

**Need:** A tidy paragraph is not proof. Your **textbook title and topic** are proof.

**Logic:** If you would not trust a stranger on the bus who claims to know your syllabus, do not blindly trust a chat that has never opened your school diary.

**Common doubt:** *“If I copy AI into my homework, will the teacher know?”* Often yes. More important: you will not know the topic in the exam hall, where the chat is not sitting next to you.

**Check rule (write this down):**

1. Ask AI to **explain** or to **give practice**, not to “do the homework.”
2. Open the **textbook** and tick what matches.
3. If something clashes, believe the **teacher and the book** until you ask in class.

## AI in Science, Commerce, Arts, and Other Fields

AI is not a third stream after Science and Commerce. It is a **helper inside every stream**.

The elephant video was one picture-guess. The same idea now sits in hospitals and on Bengaluru roads.

![Four scenes: a doctor asked to check a marked area on a scan; an elephant alert on a railway camera; a car camera boxing a cow on the road; a bus dashboard saying slow down](https://s13n-curr-images-bucket.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/othersessions/teachers-day-2026/teachersday2026-04-ai-in-three-streams.png?v=20260903b)

### Science

People who study science still need science. AI only speeds some of the looking.

- **Breast cancer screening help:** Software can look at many **breast scans** from the past. On a new scan it may mark a spot and say “doctor, please check this area.” That is a **hint**, not a report you should ask Gemini for about your own body.
- **Other health scans:** The same “mark this area” idea is used on some X-rays and other hospital pictures. The **doctor** still examines the patient and decides.
- **Weather:** The forecast on your phone is a guess from many past days and many sensors. Carry a raincoat if the sky disagrees.
- **Biology:** A leaf photo can return a name such as tulsi. You still confirm with a teacher.

**Need:** Early notice can save a life. A wrong mark can also scare a family. That is why a hospital keeps a **doctor** in the loop.

**Common doubt:** *“Will AI become the doctor?”* No. A scan helper that has never met the patient cannot replace a doctor who asks about pain, food, and history.

**Common doubt:** *“Can I upload a medical photo to Gemini?”* Do not. Health pictures are private. Screening belongs in a **hospital**, not in a class chat.

### Vehicles that notice animals

This is the elephant video on a road, not only on a railway.

- A **car or bus camera** can highlight a cow, a dog, a deer, or a person in front of the vehicle.
- The screen may say **animal detected** or draw a box around the animal — the same kind of box you saw on the railway clip.
- The driver still has to **look** and **brake**. The camera can miss an animal in rain, glare, or a crowd.

**Logic:** The system has seen many pictures of animals and people. A new frame is a new guess. It is not a person sitting on the bonnet.

### Buses and other vehicles that adjust speed

Some city buses, cars, and trains can **slow down** when they see:

- a person on a crossing
- a vehicle that stopped suddenly
- an animal on the road
- thick traffic ahead

- **Official Definition:** This is **speed help from cameras and sensors** — the vehicle reads the scene and can warn the driver or reduce speed.
- **In Simple Words:** The bus is not “driving itself like a film robot.” It is **reacting** to what the cameras notice.
- **Real-Life Example:** A Bengaluru bus approaches a crossing. The dashboard shows **Slow down**. The driver still holds the wheel.

**Common doubt:** *“So I can look at my phone in an auto because AI will brake?”* No. These systems **help**. They fail in rain, on broken roads, and when a child runs from the side. The human in charge still watches.

### Commerce

Shops, banks, and accounts already run on phones.

![Three commerce scenes: a UPI fraud alert that says do not share OTP, a kirana counter with a stock note that milk may run out, and a shop app saying people who bought this also bought notebooks](https://s13n-curr-images-bucket.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/othersessions/teachers-day-2026/teachersday2026-06-ai-in-commerce.png?v=20260903c)

- **UPI and cards:** Apps flag a payment that **looks like** fraud, which is why you see “do not share OTP.” The flag is a guess, and you still do not share the OTP.
- **Billing and stock:** A kirana or supermarket system can guess which item will run out. The owner still orders the sacks.
- **Accounts and tax support:** Software can sort bills into groups. A commerce student still needs to know **what a bill means**.
- **Online stores:** “People who bought this also bought…” is the same idea as YouTube’s next video.

If you choose Commerce, using AI well means: read the alert, do not blindly click, and still learn the subject.

### Arts

Language, writing, and school displays already use helpers — including the **Stitch** poster you watched.

![Arts helpers: a Kannada-to-English translation on a phone, a School Annual Day poster draft on a tablet, a suggested next song, and a short poem in a notebook](https://s13n-curr-images-bucket.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/othersessions/teachers-day-2026/teachersday2026-07-ai-in-arts.png?v=20260903c)

- **Languages:** Kannada to English and English to Kannada for a poem, a notice, or a dialogue. You still judge whether the tone is right.
- **School posters and projects:** Stitch or a slide deck can give a **first layout**. You still write the correct Water Cycle steps and your own title.
- **Music and film:** Next song, next show, and some background effects are suggested by patterns of what people played before.
- **Writing:** AI can offer a first draft. An arts student still owns the voice, the feeling, and the honesty of the work.

**Common doubt:** *“If AI can write a poem, why should I?”* Because the marks, the audience, and your own thinking are yours. A copied poem is not your art.

### Other fields you already see

| Field | What AI may do | What humans still do |
|---|---|---|
| **Railways** | Notice an elephant or a person on the track (E-Eye style) | Driver and staff act on the warning |
| **Road safety** | Notice an animal; ask a bus or car to slow down | Driver watches the road |

## AI and Your Career

You are in school. You do not have to pick an “AI job” this week. You do have to know that **almost every job will sit next to some AI tool**.

Keep your stream. Add the habit of clear questions and careful checking. The work is not only “computer engineer.”

A doctor can read a **scan helper** mark and still examine the patient. A driver can see **animal detected** and still watch the road. A student can use **Stitch** for a first poster and still check the science.

### What is likely to change

- Routine typing, first drafts, and first searches will get faster.
- People who **only copy** will have less to offer.
- People who **understand the subject** and use AI as a helper will finish more practice in the same evening.

### What is not going away

- Teachers, nurses, electricians, farmers, lawyers, artists, and shop owners will still be needed.
- Trust, honesty, and showing up still matter. AI has none of those on its own.
- Class 9 to 12 basics — language, maths, science, social science — are the ground. AI cannot study them **for** you.

### If you like Science

You can later work in health, environment, engineering, or research, **with** AI tools in the lab and the hospital. You still need Physics, Chemistry, Biology, or Maths.

### If you like Commerce

You can later work in accounts, banking, business, or shops, **with** billing software and fraud alerts. You still need to know money, tax rules, and people.

### If you like Arts

You can later work in language, media, design, teaching, or civil services, **with** translation and draft helpers. You still need to read, write, and think.

**Common doubt:** *“Will AI take all jobs?”* A more honest line is this: **people who use AI may do the same job faster.** The student who never learns the subject, and only copies, is the one at risk — with or without AI.

**Need:** You do not need a laptop lab today to start. Curiosity, clear questions, and checking answers are career skills already.

## Using AI Safely

A useful tool can still cause harm if you hand it the wrong thing.

![Four safety reminders: do not share a school ID, never share OTP, confirm a principal fee message in school, and pause a deepfake video and ask an adult](https://s13n-curr-images-bucket.s3.ap-south-1.amazonaws.com/othersessions/teachers-day-2026/teachersday2026-08-use-ai-safely.png?v=20260903c)

**Do not share**

- Phone number, home address, Aadhaar, school ID photo, OTP, or bank details
- Private photos of family that you would not pin on the school notice board
- A classmate’s personal story “for fun”

**Do not believe blindly**

- Medical advice, legal advice, and “guaranteed exam tips” from a chat
- A video that looks real. A **deepfake** is a video or audio that looks like a real person but was built by software. If a clip of a teacher or a leader says something shocking, pause and ask a trusted adult.

**Do not cheat**

- Exams and internal tests are for *your* head. AI stays outside the hall.
- Homework is practice. Copying a full answer trains the chat, not you.

**Do pause on money messages**

If a WhatsApp note claims to be the principal and asks for fees on a personal number, that is a **scam pattern**. AI can write fluent English, and scammers use that fluency — confirm in person or on the school’s real number.

**Common doubt:** *“Is it safe to use Gemini for Science doubts?”* Yes, for explanation and practice, if you do not paste personal data, and if you check the book.

## Key Takeaways

- **AI** is a helper that guesses from patterns. Finger unlock, face unlock, and word prediction are already on your phone.
- A **calculator** only follows a fixed rule. The **elephant video** shows the extra step: the camera tries to say *what* it saw, then warns a person.
- The same picture-guess appears in **breast-scan helpers**, **vehicles that notice animals**, and **buses that slow down**. A doctor or a driver still decides.
- A **clear question** is the skill. Watch Gemini, Lens, **NotebookLM**, **Google Stitch**, and a **school to-do app** made on Lovable or Bolt, then reuse the same strip at home.
- Use AI as a **study partner**, not as a copy machine. Your career can stay in the stream you love if you keep the subject and add the habit of asking clearly and checking.

As you move through higher classes, you will see more tools with the word AI on the label. The tools will change. The habits from this session — name the guess, ask clearly, check the answer, keep people in charge — will still be the right ones.

## Important Terms Used

| Term | Meaning in this session |
|---|---|
| **Artificial Intelligence (AI)** | A computer or phone that notices patterns in many examples and then makes a useful guess |
| **Pattern** | What usually happens next, from many past examples |
| **Finger unlock** | Opening a phone by matching your fingertip |
| **Face unlock** | Opening a phone by matching your face |
| **Word prediction** | The keyboard suggesting the next word |
| **Prompt** | The question or instruction you type or speak to an AI chat |
| **Google Gemini** | Google’s AI chat for questions, explanations, and practice |
| **Google Lens** | Camera tool that reads text, objects, and some problems from a picture |
| **Voice typing** | Speaking so the phone types the words |
| **Google Translate** | Turning text or speech from one language into another |
| **E-Eye** | Railway cameras that recognise an elephant and can warn the driver |
| **NotebookLM** | Google study tool that turns *your* notes into slides or a study guide |
| **Google Stitch** | Google tool that draws a first poster or layout from a short description |
| **Published web app** | A small page the mentor creates and puts on the internet (**Vercel**) so anyone with the link can open it |
| **Scan helper** | Software that marks a spot on a hospital picture — for example in breast-cancer screening — for a doctor to check |
| **Deepfake** | A video or audio that looks real but was built by software |
| **Check rule** | Explain or practise with AI, then confirm with the textbook and the teacher |
