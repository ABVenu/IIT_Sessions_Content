# Lecture Script: AI in Daily Life

**Session duration:** 2 hours 15 minutes  
**Audience:** Classes 9–12; government-school hall; no student laptops; pen and paper only; Kannada-speaking industry mentor preferred

**How to use this file:** This document is for **timing and facilitation only**. It is not a transcript or textbook. Use the numbered blocks to pace the room, manage the projector and your phone/laptop, and trigger participation. Definitions, tables, prompts, images, and the elephant-video embed live in **Lecture Notes.md** — project headings and images; do not read every bullet aloud. Speak in **Kannada** for the room; keep **English on the screen** for the tools they will type later.

**Break rule:** After **roughly 60–75 minutes** of session clock time (after **How AI Makes a Guess**), take **one** pause of **5–8 minutes**, then continue. Do **not** list the break as a numbered block.

**Before students sit (not a teaching block):**

- Projector + sound on. Tabs ready: Lecture Notes (elephant iframe), **Google Gemini**, **Google Lens** (phone mirrored if possible), **NotebookLM** with one printed chapter already uploaded, **Google Stitch**, **Lovable** ([lovable.dev](https://lovable.dev)) *or* **Bolt** ([bolt.new](https://bolt.new)) already signed in.
- Copy the **My School To-Do** prompt from Lecture Notes into the clipboard.
- Physical props: your unlocked phone, one English textbook page, one Kannada line, one printed maths word problem.
- Confirm the elephant video plays (iframe or [new-tab link](https://coding-platform.s3.amazonaws.com/dev/lms/tickets/4c52f3b8-4507-47f7-86c2-326d41cb325e/1UbaIsJ3NgtZNFL0.mp4)).
- Students need a notebook and pen only. Do **not** pass around laptops.

---

## 1. Welcome and What You Will Learn (6 minutes)

- Greet the hall in Kannada. One Teacher’s Day line: a teacher explains, checks, and cares; AI can practise with you — it does not replace a teacher.
- State the day in one breath: see AI already on a phone, watch one railway video, watch live tools, write one clear question, leave with a habit of checking.
- **Room action:** Ask everyone to write the heading **AI in Daily Life** on a fresh page. No phones required unless a teacher later shares a link.
- **Engagement — cold-call (2 students):** “Have you heard the word AI — in a film, on YouTube, or on WhatsApp?”
- **Engagement — thumbs up:** notebooks open, pens ready.

**Bridge sentence:** “AI is not only a robot in a film — it is already guessing on the phone in your house.”

---

## 2. What Is Artificial Intelligence (7 minutes)

- Project **What Is Artificial Intelligence**. Skim Official / Simple / Real-Life only — do not lecture the page.
- One line: AI looks at many examples, notices a **pattern**, then makes a useful **guess**.
- Keyboard example: after `I am going to`, the phone may suggest `school`. Nobody sits inside the phone.
- **Common doubt (30 sec):** “Is every smart machine AI?” — No. A calculator is fast and still not AI.
- **Engagement — thumbs up / down:** “Is a fan remote AI?” (Down.)
- **Cold-call:** “In one word, what does AI do — calculate, or guess?” (Guess.)

**Bridge sentence:** “You do not need a new app to meet AI — three things on a phone are enough.”

---

## 3. AI Already on Your Phone — Unlock and Next-Word (12 minutes)

- Project the **phone** image (`teachersday2026-01-ai-on-your-phone.png`).
- Three headlines only: **finger unlock**, **face unlock**, **word prediction**. One sentence each from the notes.
- **Room action — live demo:** Mirror or hold the phone to the camera / projector.
  1. Unlock with fingerprint.
  2. Lock; unlock with face.
  3. Open Messages or WhatsApp; type `I am`; wait for the suggestion chips.
- Students write the **three suggested words** they see.
- **Common doubt (30 sec):** Wrong next-word is normal — the keyboard guesses what is common, not what is true today.
- **Engagement — cold-call (2 students):** Read their three words aloud.
- **Engagement — thumbs up:** everyone wrote at least one suggested word.

**Bridge sentence:** “If the keyboard can guess, we also need a filter — what is AI, and what is only a fixed rule.”

---

## 4. This Is AI and This Is Not AI — Tick Activity (14 minutes)

- Project the **two-column** image (`teachersday2026-02-ai-vs-not-ai.png`).
- Rule in one line: learns from examples and guesses → AI; fixed rule that never changes → not AI.
- Flash the two tables at headline speed: Maps traffic / YouTube vs calculator / paint / alarm / torch.
- Bengaluru Maps line in 20 seconds: red lines are a guess from many phones, not a person painting the road.
- **Engagement — activity (individual, 5 min):** Students copy the 10-item list from notes and write **AI** or **Not AI** *before* you reveal.
- Circulate the aisles; do not turn it into groups.
- Reveal key: **AI — 1, 3, 5, 7, 9. Not AI — 2, 4, 6, 8, 10.**
- **Cold-call:** “Why is the calculator not AI?” (Always `2 + 2 = 4`; it did not learn from you.)
- **Pair-share (45 sec):** Turn to the neighbour — one item you got wrong and why.

**Bridge sentence:** “You just labelled a calculator as not AI — the next clip shows a guess from pictures on a railway, not on a phone.”

---

## 5. The Elephant Video (12 minutes)

- Project **Demonstration: The Elephant Video**.
- Say aloud, slowly: **“Watch this video first. Do not read the points until the clip is over.”**
- Play the **iframe**. If it fails, open the **new-tab** link and share that window.
- After the clip: project **What the video shows** — E-Eye names the object (elephant), then warns a person.
- Contrast **What a simple rule would have done**: a light that only knows “something large moved.”
- **Common doubt (30 sec):** A person does not stare at every camera; people still act on the warning.
- **Engagement — thumbs up:** “Did the system only say something moved, or did it try to say *what* moved?” (What.)
- **Cold-call:** “Face unlock asks ‘is this my face?’ — what does E-Eye ask?” (Is this an elephant?)

**Bridge sentence:** “The video is the method — show many pictures, notice a pattern, guess the next one.”

---

## 6. How AI Makes a Guess (6 minutes)

- Project **How AI Makes a Guess**. Kirana-milk analogy in one sentence; word prediction and E-Eye are the same idea.
- One line: AI can sound sure and still be wrong — a confident sentence is not a checked fact.
- Point forward: messy question → messy answer. That is why the next half is about *how you ask*.
- **Engagement — thumbs up:** “If the camera boxes a cow and there is no cow, who still watches the road?” (The driver.)

**→ Take the single break (5–8 minutes) here if you have hit ~60–75 minutes. Optional return prompt: “After break — tools you can actually use, then one clear question.” ←**

**Bridge sentence:** “The classroom has a projector; your practice later is a phone — here is what to open.”

---

## 7. Tools You Can Use — Pocket Tools (5 minutes)

- Project **Tools You Can Use After This Session**. Name only: **Gemini**, **Lens**, **voice typing**, **Translate**, YouTube “Up next,” Maps colours.
- One line each. Do not open all six now — Gemini and Lens get full demos next.
- **Common doubt (20 sec):** “Do I need ChatGPT?” — No. Gemini + Lens + Translate + voice is enough to start.
- **Engagement — cold-call:** “Which of these can you try on a parent’s phone this week?”

**Bridge sentence:** “Gemini is only as useful as the question you type — so we practise the ask.”

---

## 8. How to Ask a Clear Question — Gemini Live (12 minutes)

- Project the **clear-question** image (`teachersday2026-03-clear-question.png`) and the five-part strip: **Who / Need / Class / Language / Format**.
- Students copy the five words onto the **inside cover** of the notebook.
- **Room action:** Screen-share **Gemini**. Run **Question A** (`explain photosynthesis`), pause on the long reply.
- Then run **Question B** (Class 10, Bengaluru, 6 points, 3 exam questions, no answers).
- **Engagement — thumbs up / down:** “Which reply would you revise from the night before a test — A or B?”
- If time: run **C** (`help me with maths`) vs **D** (Class 9, linear equation, 4 practice questions, do not solve).
- Line to land: the tool did not become smarter; **the question** did the work.
- **Cold-call:** “Which part do students skip most — Who, or Format?” (Format.)

**Bridge sentence:** “Now you write one weak ask and one clear ask — on paper, for *your* syllabus.”

---

## 9. Activity: Rewrite One Weak Question (6 minutes)

- **Individual only.** Students pick one current topic (Science, Maths, Social Science, or English).
- They write (1) a weak one-line ask and (2) a clear ask using the five-part strip. No AI answer.
- Circulate; nudge anyone writing “give me all answers.”
- **Cold-call (2 students):** Read the *clear* version only.
- Early finishers write one line: how they will **check** (textbook, teacher, classmate).

**Bridge sentence:** “Typing is not the only way in — sometimes the doubt is already printed on the page.”

---

## 10. Google Lens on a Textbook (7 minutes)

- Mirror the phone or use laptop camera + Lens.
- Three live hits: English paragraph → simpler version; Kannada line → English; maths word problem → steps, then **check one step on the board**.
- Students write three lines from the notes: one thing in the bag, one thing outside class, reminder that Lens can misread a `6` as `0`.
- **Common doubt (20 sec):** Lens can read the question; they still solve or still check.
- **Engagement — thumbs up:** three lines written.

**Bridge sentence:** “Gemini answers a question; the next tools make something you can *show* — slides, a poster, and a to-do app.”

---

## 11. NotebookLM and Google Stitch (8 minutes)

- Project the **school tools** image (`teachersday2026-05-school-ai-tools.png`).
- **NotebookLM:** screen-share the pre-loaded chapter → generate a short slide deck or study guide. Point: this uses *your* notes, not an invented Chapter 12.
- **Google Stitch:** paste a clear poster ask (Science Day / Water Cycle / school-notice style, no fake statistics). Show the first layout. Say: draft only — add name, class, and teacher check before print.
- **Cold-call:** “If AI made the slides, when is the project still yours?” (When you chose the topic, checked facts, and can explain every box.)

**Bridge sentence:** “One more live build — a small homework to-do app from a single prompt.”

---

## 12. School To-Do App — Lovable or Bolt (10 minutes)

- Project **A small school to-do app**. Open **[lovable.dev](https://lovable.dev)** or **[bolt.new](https://bolt.new)** — already signed in.
- Paste the **exact prompt** from Lecture Notes (`My School To-Do`). Do not shorten it. Narrate: “I am pasting the same prompt you have in the notes.”
- Wait for the app. Then, on the projector: **add** one new task, **tick** one starter task, **delete** one task.
- Publish / Share if the button is there; open the link on the projector. Optional: one student phone opens the same link if a teacher allows it.
- **Common doubt (20 sec):** Nobody becomes a website engineer today — they watch one prompt become one app.
- **Engagement — thumbs up:** “Did you see a task get added and ticked?”

**Bridge sentence:** “These tools are impressive — they still fail if you treat them as a copy machine.”

---

## 13. Study Helper, Not a Copy Machine — Wrong Answer (7 minutes)

- One Teacher’s Day line again: teacher + AI, not AI instead of teacher.
- Flash **Good uses** vs **Poor uses** (explain / extra questions vs copy the homework / exam paper).
- **Room action:** Gemini — **Question E**: `Give me the summary of Chapter 12 of my Science book.` Show that it may invent a chapter.
- Then **Question F** with a real topic name from the board (example: Carbon and its Compounds).
- Students write the **check rule** (three lines from notes).
- **Cold-call:** “Who wins if the chat and the textbook disagree?” (Teacher and the book.)

**Bridge sentence:** “The same picture-guess from the elephant video now sits in hospitals, on roads, in shops, and in arts.”

---

## 14. Science, Vehicles, Commerce, Arts (10 minutes)

- Project the **scan / elephant / cow / bus** image (`teachersday2026-04-ai-in-three-streams.png`).
- **Science (2 min):** breast-scan helper marks “doctor, check this area.” Not a Gemini self-check. Do **not** upload medical photos.
- **Vehicles (2 min):** camera boxes a cow; bus can show **Slow down**. Driver still watches. Thumbs down: “Can I look at my phone because AI will brake?” (No.)
- Project **commerce** image (`teachersday2026-06-ai-in-commerce.png`) — UPI fraud alert, kirana stock, “people also bought.”
- Project **arts** image (`teachersday2026-07-ai-in-arts.png`) — translate, poster draft, next song, poem. Arts students still own the voice.
- **Pair-share (30 sec):** Name one field you might choose later — science, commerce, or arts.
- **Cold-call (3 students):** one line each — what AI *suggests* and what a *person* still decides.

**Bridge sentence:** “You do not pick an AI job this week — you keep your stream and add the habit of asking and checking.”

---

## 15. AI and Your Career (5 minutes)

- Project **AI and Your Career**. Read it as a **short talk**, not as a definition — there is no Official Definition here.
- Three pictures: doctor + scan helper; driver + animal box; student + Stitch poster.
- What changes / what does not: first drafts get faster; teachers, nurses, shop owners stay; Class 9–12 basics still matter.
- One line per stream (Science / Commerce / Arts) from the notes.
- **Engagement — thumbs up:** “Is ‘computer engineer’ the only future with AI?” (No.)
- **Common doubt (20 sec):** People who *use* AI may do the same job faster; people who only copy are the ones at risk.

**Bridge sentence:** “A useful tool can still cause harm — four safety rules, then we close.”

---

## 16. Using AI Safely (6 minutes)

- Project the **safety** image (`teachersday2026-08-use-ai-safely.png`).
- Four beats, one example each: do not share ID / OTP / address; do not believe medical or “guaranteed exam” chat; do not cheat; pause a **deepfake** and a fake principal fee WhatsApp — confirm in school.
- **Engagement — thumbs up / down:** “A WhatsApp from ‘Principal’ asking fees on a personal number — do you pay?” (Down.)
- **Cold-call:** “Where do you confirm that message?” (School office / real number / in person.)
- **Common doubt (15 sec):** Gemini for Science doubts is fine if they skip personal data and check the book.

**Bridge sentence:** “Leave with one promise on paper — one thing you will try this week.”

---

## 17. One-Week Try, Takeaways, and Close (5 minutes)

- Students write **one** line from the try-list (Lens, Gemini practice questions, NotebookLM, Stitch, voice-type, or the to-do link if you share it).
- Skim **Key Takeaways** — five bullets, not a second lecture.
- Thank teachers (Teacher’s Day). Invite 2–3 questions only.
- **Engagement — thumbs up:** one try-line is written.
- Optional: if you published **My School To-Do**, write the link on the board.

**Bridge sentence:** “The tools will change. Name the guess, ask clearly, check the answer, keep people in charge.”

---

## Timing Flex

If the hall is running late, **cut in this order** — do not skip the elephant video or the Gemini A/B demo:

1. Drop Question **C/D** (keep A/B only).
2. Drop the NotebookLM *or* Stitch demo — keep one of the two, plus Lovable/Bolt.
3. Shorten **Career** to the three pictures and one “keep your stream” line.
4. Skip **Home and travel** (not a numbered block; do not open that heading).
5. One-week try: students write the line while you speak takeaways.

If you are **ahead**, add a Kannada Gemini ask on the same photosynthesis topic, or let one volunteer open the published to-do link on a teacher’s phone.

If **sound or iframe fails**, use the new-tab elephant link immediately; do not narrate the clip from memory for more than 30 seconds before the video is playing.

If **Lovable / Bolt is slow**, show a pre-built **My School To-Do** tab you generated in setup, then still paste the prompt so students see the *same text* that built it.
